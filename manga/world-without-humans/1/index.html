Caio spent the first night inside the hollow trunk of a tree. He did not truly sleep. Every crack sounded like a step, every shadow like a claw. When the gray sun rose, he was hungry, cold, and covered in mud.

He ate half a loaf of bread and saved the rest. In his notebook, he wrote three rules: do not run without knowing where to go, do not make fire during the day, and do not trust anyone armed.

The third rule failed before noon. Near a clearing, Caio saw two elves. They were tall, beautiful, and silent, with long bows and pale leather clothes. For one second, he almost asked for help.

Then he saw what they were doing. The elves were chasing a small wounded creature, laughing whenever it stumbled. One of them shot an arrow just to cut off the victim's path. They were not hunting for food. They were playing.

Caio stepped back slowly, but his foot snapped a branch. Both elves turned their faces at the same time. Their eyes were silver and cold. One of them smiled like someone finding a new toy.

Caio ran. He remembered his own rule too late. Arrows hissed past his ears. He entered a part of the woods where the trees had red thorns and threw himself beneath them. The elves hesitated. The thorns moved on their own.

With the pocketknife, Caio cut strips from his own shirt and wrapped them around his arms so he could cross the brush without tearing his skin. An arrow struck his backpack but stayed caught in the fabric. He did not look back.

At the end of the trail, he found a low cave. Inside there were old bones and the smell of an animal, but there were also dry stones. Caio entered, pulled branches over the opening, and waited.

When night fell, the elves gave up. Caio was shaking all over. Even so, he did something important: he broke the arrow stuck in his backpack and kept the tip. In that world, any tool could become a chance.

He was not strong. He did not know how to fight. But he knew how to observe. And if he could survive one more day, maybe he would discover a way out.
