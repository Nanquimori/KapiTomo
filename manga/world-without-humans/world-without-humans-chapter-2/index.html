The next morning, Caio found old marks on the wall of the cave. They were not drawings of animals. They were arrows, circles, and a symbol repeated three times: a door inside a mountain.

He followed the marks through a narrow tunnel. The flashlight failed twice, and the darkness seemed to press against his face. When he came out on the other side, he saw stone ruins swallowed by roots. In the center stood a broken arch, just like the symbol on the wall.

The problem was the dragon. It slept curled around the ruins, smoke leaking from its nose. Between its scales were scars from spears and arrows. Even the elves and goblins feared it.

Caio hid and thought. He could not fight. He could not run. So he did the only thing he still knew how to do better than the monsters: improvise.

He used the empty bottle, small stones, and the arrow tip to make a rattle. He tied everything to a vine and threw it far away against a fallen column. The noise echoed through the ruin.

The dragon opened one eye. Then the other. It raised its head and moved toward the sound, irritated. Caio ran low, almost crawling, toward the stone arch.

The portal did not light up. Something was missing. Caio saw a hole in the center of the base, exactly the size of the flashlight. He swallowed hard, placed the object there, and pressed the button.

The weak light became a white line. The whole arch trembled. The dragon noticed and roared. Caio felt heat reaching his back, but he did not take his hand off the flashlight.

When the portal opened, he saw for one instant the street near his home. The same pole, the same tagged wall, the same loud human world that had once seemed too ordinary.

Caio jumped. He landed on the asphalt on his knees, with his backpack torn and mud up to his neck. The portal closed behind him like a sigh.

Nobody would believe him. Maybe even he would stop believing after a few days. But inside his backpack, there was still a black scale, hot as an ember.

Caio put the scale in his pocket and looked at the sky. He was home. But now he knew one thing he would never forget: surviving was not defeating every monster. It was continuing to think when fear wanted to think for him.
