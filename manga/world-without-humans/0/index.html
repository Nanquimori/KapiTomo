Caio was fourteen and walking home from school when the ground disappeared. One second earlier there had been asphalt, a heavy backpack, and the noise of buses. The next, there was mud up to his ankles, cold air, and a forest that breathed like a huge animal.

He called for someone. Nobody answered. There were no poles, houses, cars, or human voices. Only trees that were too tall, dark leaves, and claw marks on the trunks. Caio tightened the strap of his backpack and tried not to cry.

A roar tore through the sky. Between the clouds, a winged shadow drifted slowly past. It was a dragon. Not a drawing, not a dream. A real dragon, as large as a building, searching the valley for something.

Caio ran under a giant root and held his breath. The monster landed far away, but the wind from its wings knocked branches down around him. When everything went quiet, he opened his backpack. He had one bottle of water, two loaves of bread, a notebook, a small flashlight, and a simple pocketknife he used to sharpen pencils.

It was not much. But it was everything. Caio decided he would not waste energy screaming. First he needed shelter. Then water. Then a way to understand where he was.

While walking, he found small footprints near a stream. He thought they belonged to a child until he saw an arrow stuck in a stone. Its tip was stained with green poison.

Thin laughter rose from the bushes. Goblins. Four of them, short, skinny, and armed with curved knives, surrounded the stream while sniffing the air. Caio threw himself into the mud behind a rock and covered his mouth with his sleeve.

One goblin passed so close that Caio saw its broken teeth and yellow eyes. If he moved, he would die right there. So he stayed still, counting the beats of his own heart.

When the goblins left, Caio understood the first rule of that world: everything there saw a human as food, an enemy, or something too strange to leave alive. He picked up the poisoned arrow carefully, buried the fear deep in his chest, and kept walking before night arrived.
